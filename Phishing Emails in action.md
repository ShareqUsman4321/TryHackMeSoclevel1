Red flags to look out for:
An attention grabbing subject line
Sender email address does not match actual address this might be due to the fact that the receivers email address was scraped from data lists resulting in a weird format
An unusual domain used  in to address
Potential actions you can take:
Go through the email body and investigate any buttons( by inspecting it) there. Check the link assigned to href to find out where you would be sent by clicking the button and check it using WhereGoes or other such tools

Pixel tracking: Embedding invisible images which notify sender when email is opened
Link manipulation: Masking fraudulent link with a fake tracking number

Yahoo and many other email provider automatically block images from opening to counter pixel tracking

Credential Harvesting: deploying a fake portal or site to steal credentials

Use of atypical phone number is a red flag

In blind carbon copy(bcc) the victim is not directly emailed so they are no mentioned in BCC

URL being longer than normal and more complex is a sign of a malicious redirection attempt

Look for geographical markers such as a mismatch in language used in email and sender's given location
